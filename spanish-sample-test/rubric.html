Scoring rubric reference

What Novice and Intermediate writing and speaking look like, with short Spanish examples. Use it to coach answers and to fill in the speaking checklist.

Writing & speaking levels

LevelFCPS creditWhat it sounds likeExample (Spanish)Why (English)
Novice Low—Single words and memorized phrases.Hola. Gato. Rojo.Only isolated words; no sentences yet.
Novice Mid—Lists and memorized phrases, a few very short sentences.Me llamo Sam. Me gusta el azul. Perro.Some memorized sentences, mostly lists.
Novice High1 creditSimple sentences of their own on very familiar topics, with some errors.Tengo un perro. Es pequeño y negro. Me gusta mi perro. Mi hermana tiene diez años.Several simple, complete sentences on a familiar topic; ideas are not yet connected.
Intermediate Low2 creditsCreates sentences, joins some with y / pero / porque, adds related details.Mi familia es pequeña. Tengo una hermana y un perro. Los sábados vamos al parque porque a mi perro le encanta correr. Pero mi hermana prefiere leer.Original sentences with simple connectors and details; mostly present tense.
Intermediate Mid3 creditsStrings of connected sentences; describes experiences and tells a simple story, sometimes in past or future.El verano pasado fuimos a la playa para el cumpleaños de mi papá. Primero comimos un picnic y después nadamos. El agua estaba fría, ¡pero fue genial! El año que viene voy a volver con mis primos.Connected, organized sentences with time words and past/future; errors don’t block meaning.

Speaking examples

Speaking follows the same levels. Pronunciation should be understandable, and longer, connected answers show higher levels.

A level is passed only when all four sections pass. These thresholds are this site’s own practice rules.

How the writing estimate works (in your browser)

  • Novice High: at least 2 sentences, 15+ words, and a conjugated verb.
  • Intermediate Low: 4+ sentences, 40+ words, 28+ different words, 2+ kinds of connectors (y, pero, porque, también, entonces…), 4+ verb forms, mostly correct articles.
  • Intermediate Mid: 6+ sentences, 75+ words, 45+ different words, 3+ kinds of connectors, 6+ verb forms, past or future used, few accuracy issues.
  • Missing accents and marks (esta → está, tambien → también, espanol → español, a missing ¿ or ¡) and simple agreement or ser/estar slips (los gato, la casa blanco, yo es) are shown as gentle tips, never as a fail.

How the speaking estimate works

A parent checklist (full sentences, connected ideas, understandable pronunciation, on topic, varied vocabulary, past/future or a story). 2 ticks ≈ Novice Mid, 3 ≈ Novice High, 4–5 (including “connected”) ≈ Intermediate Low, all 6 ≈ Intermediate Mid. If you record, very short recordings cap the estimate, and when the browser offers Spanish speech recognition, the number of words heard is also considered. Everything stays on your device.

Official facts used

The only official details on this site are the section format (Reading ~30 questions, 35–40 min; Writing 3 prompts, 20–25 min, up to 2,500 characters; Listening ~30 questions, 35–40 min; Speaking 3 prompts, 20–25 min), the FCPS credit tiers (Novice High = 1, Intermediate Low = 2, Intermediate Mid = 3), and the FCPS windows (Fall Nov 4–24, 2026; Spring Mar 1–18, 2027). Everything else, including the level descriptions and thresholds, is our own practice guidance.
