Step 2

The 6 detective checks

Not every company is a good money machine. Some are traps! Before you pick one, run these 6 checks — the same ones grown-up investors use. Each check below becomes a slider in the picker.

1

Enough candy money?


Grown-up word: dividend yield

If your $100 only earns you $1 a year, that's a terrible trade. We want companies that pay at least $2.50 every year for every $100 — that's a 2.5% yield. More yearly pay = better.

2

The piggy-bank test


Grown-up word: payout ratio

If a company gives away $95 of every $100 it earns, what happens when trouble comes? Nothing left in the piggy bank! We want companies that share nicely and keep some saved — giving away less than 75%.

3

Real cash, not pretend


Grown-up word: free cash flow

Some companies look rich on paper, but open the cash register and… empty! We check that real money is left over after paying all the bills — more than $1 billion of real cash.

4

The promise keeper


Grown-up word: years of dividend raises

Would you trust a friend who kept every promise for 10 years straight? Companies that raised their yearly payment 10+ years in a row are promise keepers. One missed year and they're out.

5

The growing allowance


Grown-up word: dividend growth

Candy costs more every year. If your $5 never grows, it buys less candy each year! We want the yearly payment to grow at least 5% a year — faster than prices rise.

6

No melting ice cream


Grown-up word: company value growth

Tricky one! A company could pay you $5 while the company itself shrinks — like a melting ice cream cone dripping on your hand. Sweet at first, gone later. We want the whole company growing, not melting.
