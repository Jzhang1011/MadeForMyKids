Write Better Instructions ✏️

Your writing is the whole event. Follow the workflow, check for ambiguity, then self-score.

The writing workflow

Work top to bottom. Each step answers a question your builder will otherwise have to guess at.

  1. Overview first: one or two sentences describing the whole structure before any steps.
  2. Define the base and reference orientation: which edge faces the builder; what counts as front, left, up.
  3. List pieces or subassemblies: inventory up front, or build independent sections separately and join them.
  4. Number the steps in a sensible building order — base first, then connections, then details.
  5. Specify exact connections and positions: which end, which opening, which square.
  6. Separate essentials from details: construction instructions first; nice-to-know description after.
  7. Review and revise: hunt for ambiguous words (the checker below helps) and rewrite them.

Rubric self-check

Check each box honestly. A useful instruction lets another person build the same structure — not just a similar one.

Weak vs. improved — same structure

Both describe "First Steps" (straight connector – red rod – straight connector). Only one builds it correctly.

🚫 Weak

"Put the red piece on the blue thing and add another piece beside it."

Why it fails: connectors aren't blue, "on" and "beside" don't say which opening, and the rod length is never mentioned.

✅ Improved

"Place a straight connector with its openings facing left and right. Connect a red rod (length 3) to its right opening so the rod runs left-to-right. Connect a second straight connector to the right end of the rod, openings facing left and right."

Why it works: every piece named with size, every connection traced, left/right anchored to the front edge.

Partner tip: hand your written instructions to a partner without showing them the structure — or try a timed partner practice. Every difference is a lesson about a specific sentence you wrote.
